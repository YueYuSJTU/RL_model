Reviewer 1
1、In Equation (3), the quantities Ix,Iy,IzIx​,Iy​,Iz​ must be clearly defined.

2、In Equation (5), the specific values 500 and 15° should be replaced by general threshold variables, which should be assigned numerical values with proper physical units only after the subsequent analysis. Similarly, the parameters in Equation (13), Table 2, and Table 3 also appear overly heuristic. The authors are advised to first analyse the composition of the reward function and then assign values to these variables based on a set of generalised threshold variables; otherwise, the current discussion is insufficient.
3、The formation of the sparse reward is related to the method used for evaluating the initial task opponents. However, the proposed scheme for quantifying opponent strength relies too heavily on the training capability itself and is essentially not sufficient to support the claimed quantitative relationship. Moreover, the authors need to provide an in‑depth analysis of the baseline characteristics of the opponents, even if those opponents are generated randomly. It is recommended to construct a benchmark strategy table for comparison.
4、A dynamic opponent pool is a common and necessary training technique, but most existing works only present the design scheme. The Opponent Pool Initialization, as the core component for handling generalisation capability, should be discussed and tested separately. In addition, the parameters of the goalpoint model and its necessity warrant further investigation.

5、In the Benchmark Pool Evaluation, all training parameters for every model should be reported, specifying which parameters are shared and which differ across models. In particular, missing details on network parameters make reproducibility difficult.

6、In Figure 12, the key actions should be highlighted with emphasised bounding boxes or enlarged markers for better visibility.

7、The conclusion is overly brief. Although the novelty of the paper is relatively limited, the authors should condense and present a more substantive discussion of the core results obtained from their proposed solution.
Reviewer 2
This paper presents a PPO-based simulation study combining curriculum learning, an evolving opponent population, and two policy architectures. Its useful features include component ablations, evaluation against eight benchmark policies, cross-play comparisons, and a parallel simulation implementation. The reported results suggest benefits within the selected simulation benchmark. The following comments may be helpful in improving the work:
1. Section 4.3.1 describes the eight benchmark opponents as selected snapshots from different training iterations and configurations. It does not establish whether these policies, their predecessor checkpoints, or benchmark outcomes were used during training, opponent selection, or final model selection. This is not evidence that leakage occurred, but it prevents the reader from verifying the claimed generalization.
2. In the simulation study, the baseline also uses a simpler training population, whereas the proposed framework includes separately pretrained and selected opponents. Clarify which factors and data budgets differ in each comparison, including preprocessing and pretraining costs.
3. The Introduction already discusses previous curriculum and population-based approaches, including references [19]-[26]. The manuscript should identify what new finding follows from this study beyond integrating those ideas in the chosen simulator.
4. The computational-efficiency claim is insufficiently specified.
